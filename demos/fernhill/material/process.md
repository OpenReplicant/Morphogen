# How a job runs (the office's handbook, revised March 2024)

*(Made up for Morphogen's demo engagement.)*

1. **Enquiry.** Comes in by the website form, phone, the showroom or Houzz. Dee logs it in the job sheet
   (`jobs.xlsx`, one row a job) and books a survey in the shared Google calendar.
2. **Survey.** Owen or Priya visits, measures with a tape and a laser, sketches the room on paper, takes photos
   on their phone and talks budget.
3. **Design.** Back in the office the designer draws the kitchen in SketchUp. Two or three rounds with the
   customer by email are normal.
4. **Quote.** The designer counts cabinets and worktop metres off the drawing and types them into the quote
   calculator (`Quote calculator v7.xlsx`, see quote-calculator.md). The calculator gives the price. The quote is
   written up in Word from a template and emailed as a PDF. *Aim: quote within 5 working days of the survey.*
5. **Follow-up.** The designer chases after a week. If the customer goes quiet for a month the job is marked
   lost.
6. **Deposit.** 40% on acceptance, invoiced by Dee in Xero. The job gets a fitting week, written on the
   whiteboard in the workshop.
7. **Orders.** Dee orders carcasses (from Lindale Carcass Co.), hardware and laminate or oak worktops by email;
   quartz is booked with Stonecraft once the cabinets are fitted. Order details are retyped from the quote.
8. **Workshop.** Mark plans the workshop's week on the whiteboard: doors and frames for the next fittings.
9. **Fit.** A fitting pair fits the kitchen. Their week is on the whiteboard and in a WhatsApp group. Quartz
   templating is a separate visit, fitting a separate visit about ten days later.
10. **Snagging and sign-off.** The lead fitter walks the customer round and notes snags.
11. **Final invoice.** 60% balance, raised by Dee in Xero when the lead fitter tells her the job is signed off.
